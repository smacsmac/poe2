    The playbook

    Works for any rare. Cheap, reversible steps come first and expensive, permanent ones come last, so a bad roll early only costs you a base. The Forge screen follows this order for you.

    Before you spend anything

    • List the three prefixes and three suffixes you want. The Design screen shows which side each one is on for your exact base, and what item level its top tier needs.
    • Choose the mod for your one crafted slot. Give it to the mod that's hardest to roll, or that only exists on an essence or alloy. Astrid's Creativity, a rune, opens a second crafted slot on gear that takes sockets.
    • Choose the mod for your one desecration. Save it for the hardest mod left and use it on the last open slot.
    • Loosen targets where you can. Accepting a lower tier or any resist you need, instead of one exact roll, cuts the cost a lot.
    1. 1

      Start from a magic base

      Buy one at the right item level with one prefix and one suffix you want. To make one yourself, Perfect Transmute and Greater Aug a stack of normal bases. There's no Alteration Orb, so every miss costs a base.

    2. 2

      Make it rare

      If your crafted mod comes from a Lesser, Normal or Greater Essence, use it now. It adds its mod and removes nothing. Otherwise use a Perfect Regal.

    3. 3

      Lock in the hardest side

      Slam the side with your hardest targets first, one mod at a time, using a Greater Exalted Orb with a Sinistral or Dextral Exaltation omen. If junk lands here, start over. You've only lost a base and a few orbs.

      Still need an alloy or Perfect Essence? Leave a slot open on the side its mod belongs to.

    4. 4

      Fill the other side

      Same tools. Junk is acceptable on the side you'll aim your Crystallisation omen at in step 5, because the alloy or Perfect Essence can delete it. The fewer good mods share that side, the safer step 5 is.

    5. 5

      Add the crafted mod

      Skip this if you used an essence in step 2. Activate the Crystallisation omen for the side holding the junk, then use the alloy or Perfect Essence. It deletes a random mod from that side and adds its guaranteed mod. With only junk on that side, the deletion can't hurt you.

    6. 6

      Pick the last mod

      Use a bone with the Necromancy omen for the open side, plus Abyssal Echoes for one reroll, then choose one of three at the Well of Souls. If nothing fits, Omen of Light and an Orb of Annulment remove it so you can try again.

    7. 7

      Finish

      Quality first, then Runeforge any armour you want Runic Ward on, then Divine for the numbers, then sockets and runes. Vaal last, and only if you can live with a brick.

    Rules that decide crafts

    Most bricked items break one of these.

    • One crafted mod, one desecrated modEssences and alloys share a single crafted slot. Astrid's Creativity in a socket allows a second, so socket it before that second one. Desecration has its own single slot.
    • Activate omens firstRight-click an omen to turn it on before using the orb. Different omens stack on one orb. Two of the same kind don't.
    • Adding is cheap, removing isn'tTargeted removal omens cost 10 to 20 div each. Restart early instead of fixing late.
    • Greater and Perfect only raise tiersThey set a minimum mod level for the new mod. They don't make any particular mod more likely.
    • Whittling reads item levelIt deletes the mod with the lowest required item level, which isn't always the worst mod. Hover the Chaos Orb to see which one is marked.
    • Alloys and Perfect Essences always deleteA Crystallisation omen picks the side. The deletion is only certain when that side has one mod you'd lose.
    • Desecrate into an empty slotOn a full item, the bone deletes a random mod before adding its own.
    • Fracture and Vaal are permanent gamblesFracture only when every mod is a keeper. Vaal goes last.

    Why this order

    • Life and mana went first. Mana was the long shot among the prefixes, so it was rolled while a restart still only cost a base.
    • Runic Ward on gloves only comes from the Sovereign Alloy, so it took the crafted slot. Its random deletion was aimed at the suffix side while that side held only junk.
    • Two junk suffixes before the alloy was fine. The third suffix slot stayed empty, so the alloy was certain to delete junk.
    • The hardest suffix was saved for the desecration, where it's picked from three options instead of rolled.
    • Runeforging came last, once the mods were settled, so no Verisium went into a base that might have been binned.

    About the data

    Where the numbers in this tool come from, and how far to trust them.

    • Mods, tiers and basesPath of Building's PoE2 game data (dev branch, October 1, 2026): every base, which mods can roll on it, each tier's values and level, essences, alloys and desecrated (lich) mods.
    • Pricespoe.ninja averages for Forbidden Rites and Runes of Aldur, October 4–5, 2026. Switch league at the top. Prices move daily, so check before a big buy.
    • Odds are roughThe game data doesn't publish spawn weights, so every eligible tier counts the same. Real odds favour common mods and low tiers, so treat a "1 in 20" as "about that, often worse for top tiers".
    • Check in gameHover the orb and the Well of Souls options before you commit. The game's tooltip always wins over this tool.
